# DM Playbook — Networking & Outreach

> Real relationships drive growth faster than content alone. This is how to build them without being weird about it.

---

## The Philosophy

DMs are for building genuine relationships, not for pitching. Nobody wants a cold DM about your app. Everyone wants a real conversation with someone who gets what they're going through.

The goal: by week 8, you should have 10-15 founders who know your name, your product, and would reshare your launch tweet without being asked.

---

## DM Rules

1. **Never DM someone you haven't engaged with publicly first.** Reply to their posts 3-5 times over a week before sliding into DMs. They should recognize your name.
2. **Never pitch in the first DM.** Or the second. Or probably the third. The app comes up naturally or not at all.
3. **Keep it short.** 2-3 sentences max for first contact. Nobody reads a wall of text from a stranger.
4. **Be specific about why you're reaching out.** "I love your work" is meaningless. "Your thread on SwiftData migration saved me 4 hours last week" is real.
5. **Give before you ask.** Share something useful, offer feedback on their project, or just be genuinely interested in what they're doing.
6. **Don't mass-DM.** 2-3 thoughtful DMs per day max. Quality over quantity.

## DM Templates by Situation

### Reaching Out to a Fellow Builder

> **Context:** They're at a similar stage, building something with AI or in health/mobile space. You've replied to their posts a few times.

**Example:**

> hey, been following your build for a few weeks — the way you handled [specific thing they did] was smart. im building a health tracking app solo too and keep running into similar decisions
>
> no ask or anything, just wanted to say your posts have been helpful while im figuring things out

**What NOT to send:**

> Hi! I'm Keshav and I'm building Symptly, an AI-powered health tracking app. I noticed we're in similar spaces. Would love to connect and explore potential synergies!

(that reads like a linkedin bot and will get ignored 100% of the time)

---

### After They Engage With Your Content

> **Context:** They replied to one of your posts or liked a few things. The door is open.

**Example:**

> appreciate the reply on that thread — you're right that [reference their specific comment]. been thinking about it since
>
> what are you working on right now? saw you mention [thing from their timeline]

---

### Asking for Feedback (Only After Relationship Exists)

> **Context:** You've been chatting for a few weeks. They know what you're building. Time to get honest feedback.

**Example:**

> hey quick thing — been working on symptom logging and im stuck on whether the 4-step flow is too many taps or if each step actually matters for data quality. would you mind looking at a screenshot and telling me what you think?
>
> totally fine if youre busy, no pressure

**Why this works:** It's specific, it's easy to say no to, and it shows you value their opinion on a concrete problem — not a vague "what do you think of my app."

---

### Reaching Out to a Health Advocate

> **Context:** Someone who posts about chronic illness, symptom management, or health tracking. You've engaged with their health content empathetically for at least a week.

**Example:**

> your posts about tracking migraine triggers have been really eye-opening for me. im building a symptom tracking app and honestly your experience shaped how i think about the trigger analysis feature
>
> not trying to pitch anything — just wanted you to know your perspective matters to how this thing gets built

**What NOT to send:**

> Hi! I built an app that could really help with your migraines! Check it out at [link]

(never. ever. especially to someone sharing personal health experiences.)

---

### Networking with a Bigger Account

> **Context:** Someone with 10k-100k followers in your niche. You've been replying to their posts consistently for 2+ weeks.

**Example:**

> hey — been engaging with your posts for a while and your take on [specific topic] really shifted how i approach [specific thing in your work]. just wanted to say thanks, your content is genuinely useful not just "content"
>
> building a health tracking app if you ever want to chat about [overlapping topic]

**Key:** Bigger accounts get dozens of DMs. Yours needs to be specific and low-pressure. No ask in the first message. Ever.

---

### Proposing a Collaboration

> **Context:** You've been chatting with someone for 3+ weeks. There's a natural overlap. The collab should benefit both of you.

**Example:**

> been thinking — would you be down for a mutual thread thing? i could write about [your expertise] from my perspective building symptly and you could write about [their expertise] from yours. we tag each other and both our audiences see it
>
> no pressure at all, just thought it could be fun and useful for both of us

---

### Following Up After a Good Conversation

> **Context:** You had a great DM exchange or Twitter Space conversation. Keep the relationship warm.

**Example:**

> hey just wanted to follow up on what you said about [topic] — tried it this week and it actually worked way better than what i was doing before. the [specific detail] was the key insight
>
> hope your launch prep is going well

## Launch Amplification Prep (Phase 4)

By week 9, you should have a list of people who will amplify your launch. Don't ask them to retweet — ask them to try the app and share their honest reaction.

**The ask (only to people you've built real relationships with):**

> hey, symptly is launching [date]. i know you care about [health tracking / indie dev / whatever connects you]. would you be down to try it out before launch and share your honest take? good or bad, i just want real feedback from people i trust
>
> no obligation at all. and genuinely, if it sucks, tell me

## Things That Kill DM Relationships

- Sending a follow-up before they respond to your first message
- Making everything about your product
- Sending voice notes to someone you don't know well
- Asking for retweets, follows, or "support"
- Copy-pasting the same DM to multiple people (they talk to each other and will find out)
- Going from 0 to "let's collab" in one message
- DMing someone right after they posted something vulnerable to pitch your product as a solution
