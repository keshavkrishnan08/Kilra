# Twitter Post Formats — Personal Brand Skeleton

> For Keshav Krishnan building Symptly. Focus: building in public, vibe coding, AI tools, health tech, real human empathy.

---

## Two Non-Negotiable Rules

### 1. Quantify Everything

Numbers stop the scroll. The human brain processes numbers faster than adjectives. If you don't have exact numbers, estimate. Specific beats vague every single time.

| Weak (no numbers) | Strong (quantified) |
|---|---|
| "worked on the ML engine today" | "spent 4 hours on the ML engine today" |
| "the wellness score is pretty accurate" | "wellness score predictions match expected risk 73% of the time" |
| "lots of symptoms to track" | "30+ symptoms across 7 categories" |
| "been building for a while" | "day 87 of building symptly" |
| "got some new followers" | "went from 340 to 410 followers this week" |
| "the app loads fast" | "launch to home screen in 1.4 seconds" |
| "AI saved me time" | "claude wrote in 20 minutes what would've taken me 6 hours" |
| "users like the doctor prep feature" | "3 out of 4 beta testers said doctor prep was their favorite feature" |

**When you don't have a number, estimate honestly:**
- "probably spent ~3 hours on this" is better than "spent a long time"
- "maybe 1 in 5 entries have triggers attached" is better than "some entries have triggers"
- "i think around 60% of my code was AI-assisted" is better than "a lot of my code was AI-assisted"

**Where to put numbers for maximum impact:**
- In the hook (first line): "4 hours. thats how long it took to fix a 3-line bug"
- As the punchline: "all of this runs on a phone. zero cloud servers. zero API calls."
- In contrasts: "went from 104/100 to actually clamped between 5-95"

### 2. Be Vulnerable First, Impressive Second

Your brand isn't "look how smart I am." It's "look how honest I am about building something hard." Vulnerability is your unfair advantage — most tech accounts are performing confidence. You're showing the real thing.

**Vulnerability hierarchy (most to least powerful):**
1. Admitting you don't know the answer and are figuring it out
2. Sharing a specific failure with the emotions attached
3. Saying something scared you or made you doubt yourself
4. Acknowledging a decision might be wrong
5. Asking for help openly

**How to be vulnerable without being pitiful:**
- Always pair the struggle with action: "i dont know if this approach is right, but im shipping it and will adjust based on data"
- Show the feeling, then show what you did about it: "stared at the screen for 20 minutes feeling stuck. then opened a blank file and started the simplest possible version"
- Never ask for sympathy. Just share what happened. People will give you support on their own.

**Examples:**

> ngl the wellness score algorithm broke my brain for 2 days. kept returning scores over 100 which... isnt how percentages work. the fix was embarrassingly simple. still not sure if i should admit that publicly
>
> (i just did)

> some days i wonder if anyone actually needs this app or if im just building something for myself and hoping. day 47 of finding out

> i mass-accepted AI suggestions without reading them carefully and spent the next 3 hours untangling what went wrong. lesson learned the hard way: review every line. every single one.

## 20 Winning Post Formats

### Build In Public

**1. The Daily Ship Log**
> Just shipped [feature]. Took [time]. Used [tool]. Here's what I learned...
>
> Best for: showing consistent momentum. Attach a screenshot when possible.

**2. The Before/After**
> Left: what it looked like yesterday. Right: what it looks like now.
> One line about what changed and why.
>
> Best for: visual proof of progress. Always include side-by-side images.

**3. The Honest Struggle**
> Been stuck on [problem] for [time]. Tried [approach 1], [approach 2]. Nothing worked until I...
>
> Best for: vulnerability that builds trust. People root for you when you're real about failure.

**4. The Weekly Recap**
> Week [X] building Symptly:
> - shipped [thing]
> - broke [thing]
> - learned [thing]
> - next week: [thing]
>
> Best for: every Friday or Monday. Consistency builds audience habit.

**5. The Metric Drop**
> [Number] [metric] in [timeframe].
> Here's what I think drove it: [insight].
> Here's what I'd do differently: [honesty].
>
> Best for: credibility. Even small numbers work when framed as a journey.

### Vibe Coding / AI Tools

**6. The Tool Reveal**
> I just [built/designed/fixed] [thing] using [AI tool] in [time].
> This would've taken me [longer time] manually.
> The future of building is [observation].
>
> Best for: tapping into the AI builder audience. High shareability.

**7. The Prompt Screenshot**
> Here's the exact prompt I gave Claude to [outcome]:
> [screenshot or quote]
> And here's what it generated: [result]
>
> Best for: practical value. People save and bookmark these.

**8. The AI Limitation Post**
> AI is incredible at [thing]. But here's where it completely fell apart today:
> [honest story about a failure or hallucination]
> What I did instead: [human solution]
>
> Best for: nuanced takes that stand out from pure hype.

**9. The Stack Reveal**
> My entire tech stack for building a full iOS app solo:
> - [tool] for [purpose]
> - [tool] for [purpose]
> ...
> Total monthly cost: $[X]
>
> Best for: list posts get high saves. Update and repost quarterly.

### Personal Brand / Founder Journey

**10. The Origin Story**
> [X] months ago I was [previous state].
> Today I [current state].
> Here's what changed: [turning point]
>
> Best for: milestone moments. Use sparingly (monthly max) for impact.

**11. The Unpopular Opinion**
> Unpopular opinion: [contrarian take about your industry]
> Here's why I think this...
>
> Best for: sparking debate. Engagement magnet. Don't be contrarian for its own sake — believe what you say.

**12. The Personal Lesson**
> Something I wish I knew before I started building:
> [lesson]
> Would've saved me [time/pain/money].
>
> Best for: wisdom posts that feel earned, not preachy.

**13. The Gratitude / Shoutout**
> Shoutout to @[person] for [specific thing they did].
> [Why it mattered to you personally]
>
> Best for: building genuine relationships. Tag people. They'll engage back.

### Health Tech / Domain Expertise

**14. The "Did You Know" Health Insight**
> Most people don't realize: [health stat or insight]
> That's why I built [feature] into Symptly.
> Your body is giving you signals — you just need to track them.
>
> Best for: positioning as a domain expert. Tie every health insight back to the app naturally.

**15. The User Story (Anonymous)**
> Someone told me they used Symptly to [outcome].
> They said: "[quote or paraphrase]"
> This is why I build.
>
> Best for: social proof and emotional resonance. Even early beta feedback counts.

**16. The Problem Statement**
> [X million] people deal with [health problem].
> Most of them [current bad solution].
> I'm building something better. Here's my approach:
>
> Best for: framing the mission. Works for new followers who don't know your story yet.

### Engagement / Community

**17. The Poll / Question**
> What's harder about managing your health:
> A) Remembering to track symptoms
> B) Explaining symptoms to your doctor
> C) Finding patterns in how you feel
> D) All of the above
>
> Best for: algorithm boost. Polls get high engagement. Ask questions your audience actually cares about.

**18. The Hot Take Thread**
> I spent [X] hours looking at how health apps handle [thing].
> Here's what most of them get wrong:
> (thread 🧵)
>
> Best for: long-form authority building. Threads get 3x engagement of single tweets.

**19. The "What Would You Do"**
> I'm deciding between [Option A] and [Option B] for Symptly.
> Which would you prefer as a user? Here's the tradeoff:
>
> Best for: involving your audience in decisions. They feel invested in your product.

**20. The Raw Moment**
> No filter: [raw honest thought about building, health, life]
> Sometimes building alone is [emotion].
> But then [thing happens] and it's worth it.
>
> Best for: the posts that feel unplanned. Ironically, these often perform best.

## Hook Strategies

The first line decides everything. Here's how to stop the scroll:

### Pattern Interrupt Hooks
- "I accidentally [unexpected outcome] and here's what happened"
- "This is going to sound weird but..."
- "I deleted [thing everyone uses] and my [metric] improved"
- "Nobody talks about this part of building an app"

### Curiosity Gap Hooks
- "The one thing I changed that [dramatic result]"
- "I found something in my health data that surprised me"
- "Here's why I stopped [common practice]"
- "99% of health apps do this wrong"

### Emotional / Empathy Hooks
- "I built this because nobody listened when I said I wasn't feeling well"
- "Some days building feels pointless. Today was one of them. Then..."
- "My mom asked me why her symptoms keep changing. I didn't have an answer. So I built one."
- "The hardest part of chronic illness isn't the pain — it's nobody believing you"

### Authority / Data Hooks
- "I analyzed [X] data points and found..."
- "After [X] months of building, here's what actually works"
- "I tested [X] different approaches to [thing]. Only one worked."
- "[Specific number] — that's how many [thing]. Here's why it matters."

### Direct / Conversational Hooks
- "Real talk:"
- "Here's something I haven't shared yet"
- "Let me show you something"
- "Quick thread on [topic] because people keep asking"

### Contrarian Hooks
- "Unpopular opinion:"
- "Hot take: [take]"
- "Everyone says [common advice]. I think they're wrong."
- "The worst advice I got about [topic]"

## Body Strategies

### The Story Arc
Open with conflict → show the struggle → reveal the insight → land the lesson. Works for any post longer than 2 sentences. Don't lecture. Narrate.

### The List Drop
3-5 bullets of value. No fluff, no filler. Each bullet should be saveable on its own. End with one that hits different from the rest.

### The Show Don't Tell
Screenshot, video, or code snippet first. Let the visual do the talking. Your text just adds context. "Here's what happened when I..." beats "I think that..." every time.

### The Conversational Riff
Write like you're texting a friend. Short sentences. Fragments. Line breaks between thoughts. Let the reader breathe. This is how real people talk online.

### The Data Story
Lead with a number. Explain the context. Show what it means. Make the reader feel something about the data, not just read it.

### The Vulnerability Layer
Share the emotion, not just the event. "I shipped the feature" is boring. "I shipped the feature at 2am after three failed attempts and honestly I'm not sure it's good enough" is human.

## Conclusion / CTA Strategies

### The Soft Ask
> "If you're building something too, I'd love to hear about it."
> "Drop your project below — I'll check it out."

### The Follow Hook
> "I'm sharing the whole journey here. Follow along if you want."
> "More of this coming. Hit follow so you don't miss it."

### The Question Close
> "What would you build if you had unlimited time?"
> "Am I the only one who feels this way?"
> "What's the hardest part of managing your health?"

### The Cliffhanger
> "Tomorrow I'm revealing [thing]. Stay tuned."
> "Part 2 coming soon — this one's going to be controversial."

### The Emotional Land
> "This is why I build."
> "Some problems are worth losing sleep over."
> "If this helps even one person understand their body better, it was worth it."

### The No-CTA CTA
Sometimes the best ending is no ending. Just stop after the last point. Let the post breathe. No "follow me" or "like and RT." Just the raw thought. The audience will engage because they felt something, not because you asked them to.

## Anti-Bot Rules

Every post should feel like it came from a real person having a real moment. Here's how:

1. **Imperfect grammar is fine.** Start sentences with "And" or "But." Use fragments. Real people don't write in perfect prose on Twitter.
2. **Vary post length wildly.** Some posts: 1 sentence. Others: 12 lines. Bots are consistent. Humans aren't.
3. **Reference time and place.** "It's 11pm and I'm still tweaking this animation" > "Working on animations today"
4. **React to real things.** Quote tweet someone. Screenshot a conversation. Respond to news. Bots operate in a vacuum. Humans operate in context.
5. **Contradict yourself sometimes.** Last week you loved a tool, this week it annoyed you. That's human.
6. **Use "I" more than "you."** Personal brand means personal. Share YOUR experience before giving advice.
7. **Don't always include a CTA.** Sometimes just think out loud. Not everything needs to convert.
8. **Typos happen.** Don't correct every single one. Leave the occasional "teh" or missing period. (Don't overdo this — just don't obsess over perfection.)
